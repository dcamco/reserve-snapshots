What 807 comments reveal

Every comment on the last ten BaT manual auctions, classified by topic.
  • Color is the hidden price variable. Spec praise was the #1 topic by a wide margin. The crowd goes audibly feral for any manual 4.7 that isn't black or silver — and the two strongest ordinary-car results of the summer were both color-story cars. In a market this cheap, spec scarcity is what separates a $47k result from a $60k+ one.
  • The clutch is the PPI battleground. Nearly every thread litigates clutch wear, and bidders forensically parse hedged seller language until it's retracted or proven. Twin-disc (4.7) vs single-disc (4.3) feel, replacement cost (~$3–4k), and "original or replaced?" are the standard gauntlet.
  • Owners are evangelizing at the bottom. Multiple current owners posted unprompted that the cars are badly under-valued — one directly comparing build quality favorably against a Ferrari 360 he'd just sold. Owner-cohort evangelism while prints rise is the classic early-re-rate signature.
  • The extinction narrative has arrived. Naturally aspirated, hydraulic steering, no hybrid assistance, a real gated-adjacent 6-speed, and a successor generation that dropped the manual — commenters now recite the future-classic case as settled fact.
  • Known-issue literacy is high. The 4.3's timing-cover weep is common knowledge (asked about specifically when PPIs mention leaks), as are aging tires on low-mile cars and stuck seat modules. Sellers who answer with documents get rewarded; hedgers get repriced.
  • Lifestyle buyers are showing up. Golf-bag capacity and shipping-to-Palm-Springs questions are appearing alongside the enthusiast interrogation — demand is broadening beyond the faithful, which is how thin bottoms become durable floors.

The fine-tuned read

Where the value is A 2009–2016 4.7 manual coupe in the $42–48k band — ideally Sports Pack, a color with a story, and clutch documentation. The 4.7 fixed the 4.3's appetite, and the GT prints ($74–80.5k) show where the tier above already went. An ordinary 4.7 manual bought in the mid-$40s sits one rung below variants that have already re-rated.
What to avoid paying up for 4.3 cars are honest fun but will remain the discount tier — buy them cheap ($35–42k) or not at all, and check the timing cover. Sportshift cars keep getting cheaper for a reason: no conversion pipeline exists to rescue them, and the market has decided. Roadsters trail coupes.
Why the bottom looks real Three independent signals agree: the yearly average turned (2025 trough), the worst-print floor jumped 60%, and the late/special tier detached upward first — the same sequence the 996 Turbo and McLaren 12C followed out of their floors. Terminal supply seals it: the manual gen-1 fleet only shrinks.
The honest caveats The 2026 average is flattered by GT-car mix — ordinary cars are up a real but modest 5–10%. Running costs are genuine (~$2–3k/yr done right, clutch as a consumable), sub-3:1 parts-availability depends on a shrinking specialist network, and this recovery is one hot summer old. It's a floor with evidence, not a rocket.
